# YC-style video research → rules for the Kirchhoff submission video

Research date: Fri 16 Oct 2026. Target: Google Cloud AI Builder Cup submission video, strictly **under 3:00**, mixing real product footage, light-theme motion-graphic connector scenes (Gmail/SAP → Kirchhoff → SAP/Google Drive), and Mehul + Jayadratha speaking in natural Indian English (no American-style hype).

---

## 1. Key principles (with citations)

1. **Founders talk, unscripted, bullet points not a script.** YC's own application video page: "The video should be 1 minute long and should contain nothing except the founders talking... Do not recite a written script: Use bullet points instead. Using bullet points, just talk spontaneously as you would to a friend. Reading a written script doesn't help your application or convey communication skills." — [ycombinator.com/video/](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/)

2. **All founders on camera, together or on a screen-recorded call.** "If you have more than one founder, have all of your founders in the video. If you can't be in the same room at the same time, screen record a video call instead." — [ycombinator.com/video/](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/)

3. **What to say is simple and concrete: who you are, what and why, one honest status line.** "Introduce yourselves, explain what you're doing and why, and tell anything else you want to about the founders or the project"; the applied version adds: name + role fast, one plain-English sentence on what the company does, why you specifically (tied to something real you lived), one honest status line with a number. — [ycombinator.com/video/](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/); [nanoflick.com — 11 Tips for your 2026 YC Founder Video](https://www.nanoflick.com/resources/guides/y-combinator-founder-video)

4. **Plain setting, clean audio, no polish.** "Stand in front of a clean wall and look at the camera. Make sure the audio is clear, the lighting is decent, and the background is distraction-free. Every second spent playing with subtitles, filters or transitions is better spent talking to customers. Keep it cut and dry without music, sound effects, or other embellishments." — [nanoflick.com](https://www.nanoflick.com/resources/guides/y-combinator-founder-video)

5. **Show, don't tell — get into the product immediately.** "'Show, don't tell' is... the most important rule of demoing right after 'get into the product as soon as possible.'" Presenters who narrate a feature and then show it again waste time; "the longer it takes to show your product, the worse your product is." Best demos run under 5 minutes. — TechCrunch, "How To Demo Your Startup" (via [ycombinator.com/video/](https://www.ycombinator.com/video/) search context, original: techcrunch.com/?p=20915)

6. **The launch/demo video's one job: a stranger understands what you do and why it matters, in under ~2 minutes, without you in the room.** Needs: a founder on camera who sounds natural, real product footage, an edit that respects the viewer's attention, clarity and pace over polish ("you don't need drone shots or a cinematic color grade"). — [catch30.com — YC founders: shoot your launch video before Demo Day](https://catch30.com/blog/yc-demo-day-launch-video)

7. **One complete workflow, honestly shown, edited for clarity not appearance.** Open on the task/context, then show the product doing it end-to-end (input → behavior → result); skip logins/setup without context, repeated explanations, long pauses, extended intros; "a limited but honest demonstration is more informative than presenting unfinished functionality"; narrate *why* an action matters, not every click. — [whatastory.agency — YC Product Demo Video Best Practices](https://www.whatastory.agency/blog/yc-product-demo-video-best-practices)

8. **The first 5 seconds decide whether anyone keeps watching — but only if the hook doesn't sacrifice clarity.** Across 24 YC videos, the strongest had "an immediate reason to keep watching" while still making the product understandable; narrative order that works is problem → product demo → why it matters → clear CTA; "clear product story came first... strong scripts could carry videos even when the [production] was not perfect." — [motiontheagency.com — How Startups Use Demo Videos: 24 YC Examples Reviewed](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos)

9. **Captions matter; design to read without sound.** B2B/SaaS demo-video practice: "a good SaaS product demo video reads without sound" — add captions since a large share of video plays muted. — [flowjam.com — Startup Product Demo Video](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/startup-product-demo-video) (cross-checked against general 2026 SaaS demo-video guidance)

10. **Raw authenticity beats packaged hype.** A YC founder's retrospective: "Raw authenticity beats packaged hype every time. We learned that our audience connects far more with a founder genuinely explaining a problem and showing the raw UI solution than they do with a slick product video." — [motiontheagency.com](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos)

### Common mistakes to avoid (compiled from several founder-advice posts)
- Overproduction: added background music, filters, transitions, drone shots, cinematic color grading — all flagged as wasted effort versus substance. — [nanoflick.com](https://www.nanoflick.com/resources/guides/y-combinator-founder-video); [catch30.com](https://catch30.com/blog/yc-demo-day-launch-video)
- Reading a visible or memorized script — eyes darting off-screen, robotic delivery. — [techwisehub.com-style founder-advice synthesis, cross-referenced with ycombinator.com/video/]
- Rambling for a full minute without ever stating what the company does plainly. — [startupscience.io — How to Get Into Y Combinator](https://www.startupscience.io/articles/how-to-get-into-y-combinator)
- Buzzwords / "revolutionizing" / marketing language instead of plain description. — multiple sources above, consistent with Michael Seibel's general guidance to "use the simplest language possible" and that if he doesn't understand what a company does from the pitch, he can't fund it — [ycombinator.com/blog/michael-seibel-on-starting-a-startup-finding-product-market-fit-and-fundraising](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/michael-seibel-on-starting-a-startup-finding-product-market-fit-and-fundraising)
- Only one founder visible/speaking while the deck says "we." YC is evaluating the *team*, so every founder should get real speaking time. — [nanoflick.com](https://www.nanoflick.com/resources/guides/y-combinator-founder-video)
- Treating the video as a last-minute task: production advice recommends shooting weeks ahead of a deadline so there's slack to redo weak takes — directly applicable to our 90-minute budget, meaning we should timebox ruthlessly rather than chase a perfect take. — [catch30.com](https://catch30.com/blog/yc-demo-day-launch-video)
- Demoing with setup/login friction visible, or narrating a feature then re-showing it (double-telling). — [whatastory.agency](https://www.whatastory.agency/blog/yc-product-demo-video-best-practices)

Total budget: **2:50** (leave 10s margin under the 3:00 hard cap). Mehul and Jayadratha on camera (see §3), real Kirchhoff product footage, and the existing light-theme connector motion graphics (Gmail/SAP → Kirchhoff → SAP/Google Drive) used as connective tissue, not as the opening.

| **1:50–2:15** | Founder-on-camera beat #2 — the result / status | Back to founders on camera, one honest status line with a real number (e.g. "Today it's cut our layout-to-sign-off time from X days to Y hours on N real projects") — no inflated claims. | #3 — "one honest line... with a number if it helps." |

Notes:
- Total = 2:50, with a few seconds of slack built into each beat so edits can tighten rather than need re-shoots.
- Use captions throughout, not just on the demo section — holds for the founder segments too, since many reviewers may watch muted first.
- Keep any music, if used at all, very low and instrumental under the demo section only; do **not** put music under the founders talking (per #4, #10 — avoid "packaged").

## 3. Recording advice for Mehul and Jayadratha

**On-camera vs. photo + voice: record a short real on-camera segment.** YC's own norm, repeated across every source above, is founders physically on camera, unscripted, in a plain setting (#1, #2, #4) — a photo-plus-voiceover reads as evasive/corporate relative to that norm, and "raw authenticity beats packaged hype" (#10) is explicitly about the founder being visibly real. Given the 90-minute total budget, a 15-minute on-camera shoot is very achievable and is the right call over photo+voice.

**How to do it in 15 minutes:**

1. **Setup (3 min).** Phone (not webcam — modern phone cameras are sharper and easier to position) on a tripod or propped against something stable, landscape orientation, at eye height. Stand ~1.5–2m from camera. Plain wall or a tidy section of the office behind you — no clutter, no bright window directly behind you.
2. **Lighting (2 min).** Face a window or lamp — light should come from in front/slightly to the side of your face, never from behind. If it's daytime, position both of you facing the brightest window in the room. Avoid overhead-only fluorescent light if you can get any window light.
3. **Sound (2 min).** Record somewhere quiet (close a door, turn off AC/fan if it's loud). Use the phone's built-in mic from ~1.5m away — that's close enough if the room isn't noisy; if you have any wired earphones with a mic, even those clipped to a shirt collar improve clarity a lot. Do one 5-second test clip and play it back before the real takes.
4. **Content, not script (3 min prep).** Write 3–4 bullet points each, not sentences: (1) name + role, (2) one line on what Kirchhoff does, (3) one line on why you're building it (something real you hit), (4) one honest status line with a number. Do not write out full sentences to read — per #1, scripted delivery reads as stiff and is explicitly discouraged.
5. **Record (5 min).** Do 2–3 takes of each segment (intro together, then individual why/status lines), talking to each other or to the lens like you're explaining it to a colleague, in your normal Indian English — no need to perform an American pitch register. Pick the best take per segment afterward; don't try to get one perfect continuous take.
6. **Keep it simple.** No filters, no on-screen titles baked in by the camera app, no background music during the shoot — add captions/graphics later in editing. Per #4, every minute spent on in-camera polish is a minute not spent getting a natural, clear take.

This whole flow fits inside 15 minutes if the bullet points are prepared in advance (which the structure in §2 above already gives you).

## 4. Do / don't checklist for the final edit

**Do**
- Open on real product footage in the first 5–8 seconds — no logo card, no title slide first.
- Get both founders on camera, speaking in their own words, early in the video.
- Caption every spoken line (readable muted).
- Show each integration (Gmail, SAP, Google Drive) only at the moment it actually fires in the workflow, not as a generic montage.
- Keep the demo to one coherent, end-to-end workflow rather than a feature tour.
- Include one honest, specific status line with a real number.
- Hard-cut out dead air, login/setup friction, and repeated explanations.
- End on a plain tagline card and a single CTA; hold, don't fade-swirl.
- Check total runtime is under 3:00 with margin (target ≤2:50) before final export.

**Don't**
- Don't read a written script on camera, or let eyes dart to off-screen notes.
- Don't use hype language ("revolutionizing," "game-changing," "disruptive").
- Don't put music under the founder-talking segments.
- Don't add drone shots, heavy color grading, elaborate transitions, or filters — they cost time and signal "packaged," not authentic.
- Don't narrate a feature and then show it again (double-telling) — show it once, explain why it matters while it happens.
- Don't open with a company history, market-size slide, or feature list before the product appears.
- Don't let only one founder speak while implying "we" — both Mehul and Jayadratha need real, audible time on camera.
- Don't leave any personal data (emails, phone numbers, account IDs) visible in product-footage screenshots.
